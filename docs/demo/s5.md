# S5 demo: the Analyst review queue

The click-through for S5's part of the checkpoint (D114, D182). Follow these steps while recording
the walkthrough with the Mac's screen recorder (D115).

## Before you start

- The one-time setup is done (D65): Caddy's local certificate is trusted, and the hosts file has
  `127.0.0.1 grc.localhost`.
- **The running stack shows S5 only after the MVP-end rebuild and migration (D229).** Until then
  https://grc.localhost runs the older code, with no Analyst review screen, and the database lacks
  the processing and review migrations that `pnpm seed:demo` needs (without the review migration it
  skips its review step and says so). Run this walkthrough once the migrations are applied and the
  stack is rebuilt:
  `docker compose -f packages/infra/compose.yaml --env-file .env up -d --build`.
- bge-m3's tokenizer file is set up once on the Mac: `pnpm --filter api setup:tokenizer`. The seed
  reads the demo documents into chunks the way the worker does, and needs it. Without it the seed
  names this command, adds the findings that don't need the documents, and adds the rest on its next
  run.
- The demo logins and data exist. They come from `pnpm seed:demo`, which creates two orgs, Acme
  Corp (`@acme.example`) and Globex Ltd (`@globex.example`), with one user per role, each org's own
  records, mappings and uploads (`docs/demo/s1.md` to `s3.md`). For S5 it gives each org, with no
  call to Gemma or bge-m3, the findings processing would have left for the Analyst after reading the
  demo documents. They go through S4's own rule checks, keys and labels, so they read like real
  processing output. In Acme:
  - from the Acceptable Use Standard (`acme-acceptable-use-standard.txt`):
    - a waiting link: Endpoint detection and response governed by the Information Security Policy,
      whose sentence doesn't name the policy;
    - a live AI link, made live by the rule checks: Multi-factor sign-in for remote access governed
      by the Remote Working Policy (Gemma's "Remote access"), with its `link.created` entry;
  - from the SOC ticket file (`acme-soc-tickets-2026-09.json`):
    - a new incident proposed from ticket SOC-4471, "Ransomware behaviour blocked by endpoint
      detection on the build server", with a waiting link from it to Build server;
    - a waiting link from Phishing email reported by finance to "payroll team", where matching isn't
      sure which record is meant: Payroll team mailbox or Payrol team mailbox;
    - a waiting link in the wrong direction: Phishing of staff exposes Phishing email reported by
      finance (an incident exposes a risk, never the other way);
  - a possible duplicate: Payroll team mailbox and Payrol team mailbox (close spelling);
  - two suggested mappings: Cloud configuration monitoring satisfies ISO/IEC 27001 A.8.9
    Configuration management, and SOC 2 CC6.6 maps to NIST SP 800-53 SC-7 Boundary Protection.

  It also adds the records these need (the two mailboxes and the Remote Working Policy), and stores
  the two documents' chunks, so the worker later only embeds them. Globex gets its own set (step 12).
  Running it again adds nothing. Every finding is labelled Internal, so the Analyst, cleared for
  Internal, sees all of them.

- When the worker has read the demo documents with Gemma, its own findings join the seeded ones, so
  the numbers on the tabs may be higher than those below. The steps use the seeded findings by name.
- Every demo login uses the same password: the `DEMO_USER_PASSWORD` value in `.env`. It is never
  written here or printed.
- Have an authenticator app on your phone ready. The first sign-in of each login asks you to set up
  two-factor sign-in, as in the M0 demo (`docs/demo/m0.md`, steps 3 to 6).

The logins used below:

| Login                       | Role         | Clearance    |
| --------------------------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| `analyst@acme.example`      | Analyst      | internal     |
| `admin@acme.example`        | Admin        | restricted   |
| `risk.manager@acme.example` | Risk Manager | confidential |
| `analyst@globex.example`    | Analyst      | internal     |

## Steps

1. Open https://grc.localhost and sign in as the Analyst, `analyst@acme.example`, with the demo password.
2. Open the queue and its counts. In the left navigation, under Compliance, open Analyst review. It opens on the Links tab with the status Waiting, and each tab shows how many findings wait: after a fresh seed, Links 4, New records 1, Possible duplicates 1 and Suggested mappings 2. Each waiting link shows why it waits, in plain words: "The sentence doesn't name both records", "This kind of link doesn't fit these records" or "Not sure which record is meant", with its document, its label (Internal) and when it was found. Set Status to Live: the one link the rule checks made live is listed, Multi-factor sign-in for remote access governed by the Remote Working Policy. Set Status back to Waiting.
3. Approve a link by choosing a candidate. Open the waiting link from Phishing email reported by finance to "payroll team" (Not sure which record is meant). Copy the page's address for step 13. Its Source section names `acme-soc-tickets-2026-09.json`, a SOC ticket file uploaded by the Analyst, and shows the sentence "Staff member reported a phishing email impersonating the payroll team" highlighted in its passage. In The link, the left end, "phishing email", is matched to the existing incident Phishing email reported by finance; the right end, "payroll team", offers two candidates, each with its number, how matching found it (Close meaning) and its score: Payroll team mailbox (87%) and Payrol team mailbox (86%). Choose Payroll team mailbox, press Approve, type the note `The spoofed sender was the payroll team's mailbox` and press Approve in the dialog. The page reads "Approved. The link is now in the graph." Open Assets, then Payroll team mailbox: its page lists the incident among its related records.
4. Approve the proposed incident, then its link. Back in Analyst review, open the New records tab, then "Ransomware behaviour blocked by endpoint detection on the build server" (a new incident, with 1 link waiting on it). Press Approve. The form "Approve as a new incident" is filled in from the ticket: the name, Severity High, Incident status Closed and Occurred at 3 September 2026, 08:14 UTC (shown in your own time); the ticket number SOC-4471 is listed as not used. The label can't be lower than the document's, Internal, or higher than your clearance, Internal. The form also lists the 1 link waiting on this proposal. Press Approve and create incident. The page reads "Approved. INC… was created from this proposal. The waiting link that points to it is now ready to approve." Open that link from the list on the page: its left end now shows the proposed record as Approved, and its right end is matched to Build server. Press Approve, then Approve in the dialog: "Approved. The link is now in the graph." Open the new incident's page (Open INC… on the proposal's page, or from Incidents): its related records list Build server.
5. Merge the duplicate. Open the Possible duplicates tab, then Payroll team mailbox and Payrol team mailbox (Close spelling, 95%). Both records are shown with their owner, links and source IDs; the older one, Payroll team mailbox, is chosen to keep. Press Merge: the confirmation "Merge AST… into AST…?" says Payrol team mailbox is retired into Payroll team mailbox, its source ID moves to Payroll team mailbox, the kept record takes the higher of the two labels (Internal), and it can't be undone. Press Merge records. The page reads "Merged. AST… is kept, and AST… is retired into it." In Assets, Payrol team mailbox is no longer in the active list.
6. Approve a mapping and see it on the control page. Open the Suggested mappings tab, then Cloud configuration monitoring satisfies A.8.9 Configuration management. The mapping panel shows both ends and the AI's short reason. Press Approve, then Approve in the dialog: "Approved. The mapping is now in the graph." Open Controls, then Cloud configuration monitoring: its "Requirements this control satisfies" section lists A.8.9 under ISO/IEC 27001, marked as found by the AI (hover it: "Found by the AI. It is changed only through the Analyst's review."). Leave the SOC 2 CC6.6 suggestion waiting.
7. Reject a finding. Back in Analyst review, on the Links tab, open the link Phishing of staff exposes Phishing email reported by finance ("This kind of link doesn't fit these records": an incident exposes a risk, never the other way round, and approving it would be refused). Press Reject, type the note `Wrong direction; the incident already exposes this risk`, and press Reject in the confirmation. The page reads "Rejected. The finding is kept but no longer waits, and it won't be raised again." Set Status to Rejected on the queue to see it there.
8. Remove the live link as a false positive. Set Status to Live and open Multi-factor sign-in for remote access governed by the Remote Working Policy. "Why it went live" shows that every rule check passed: the sentence "Remote access to Acme systems requires multi-factor sign-in." names both ends, the kind of link fits, and both names matched a record. But the sentence is a rule of the Acceptable Use Standard; it doesn't say the Remote Working Policy governs this control. Press Remove (false positive), type the note `The standard's own rule, not the remote working policy`, and press Remove. The page reads Removed, and Multi-factor sign-in for remote access no longer lists the Remote Working Policy among its related records. This is the only way an AI link ever leaves the graph (D207).
9. Remove everything from one upload. Suppose the September SOC export was a bad one. Open the New records tab, set Status to Approved and open the new incident's finding from step 4. In its Source section press All findings from this upload: the queue narrows to `acme-soc-tickets-2026-09.json` and shows This upload above it, with what it put in. For the seeded findings that is 2 live AI links in the graph (the two links approved in steps 3 and 4), 1 record approved from it (the new incident), no waiting findings, and 3 live or approved findings. Press Remove everything from this upload: the confirmation names the document and its uploader and shows the same counts. Tick "I understand this can't be undone", type the note `Bad export from the SOC tool`, and press Remove everything. It reads "Everything from this upload was removed" with what was removed. The new incident is retired (Incidents no longer lists it as active), both links are gone from the graph, the upload's findings read Rejected or Removed, and in Data intake the file reads Removed. Uploading the same file again doesn't bring it back.
10. Sign out, and sign in as the Admin, `admin@acme.example`. Open Analyst review: a note says "You can follow every finding and how it was decided, read-only. Only an Analyst approves or rejects them." The tabs, counts and findings are the same, and the Admin sees findings at every label. Open the Suggested mappings tab, set Status to Approved and open the mapping approved in step 6: its Decision section shows the Analyst as the decider, with the time and outcome, and there is no Approve, Reject or Remove button anywhere. On a finding from the SOC ticket file, the upload's counts show, without the Remove everything button.
11. Sign out, and sign in as the Risk Manager, `risk.manager@acme.example`. Open Analyst review: "You don't have access to Analyst review". Analysts decide these findings and Admins follow them; every other role is refused, here and by the API (D50).
12. Sign out, and sign in as the Analyst of the other org, `analyst@globex.example`. Open Analyst review. The queue lists only Globex's own findings, none of Acme's: the waiting links from the Driver Device Standard (Mobile device management for scanners governed by the Mobile Device Policy, whose sentence doesn't name the policy; and "scanner" exposed to Lost or stolen driver scanners, with three candidates: Driver handheld scanners, Spare scanner pool and Spare scaner pool) and from the SOC ticket file (the new incident proposed from ticket GSOC-0912, "VPN sign-ins for one driver account from two countries within an hour", with its link to VPN gateway; and Dispatch office PCs impacts Malware found on a dispatch PC, the wrong direction); the possible duplicate Spare scanner pool and Spare scaner pool; the suggested mappings Uptime monitoring and alerting satisfies ISO/IEC 27001 A.8.16 and SOC 2 CC7.4 maps to NIST SP 800-53 IR-4; and, with Status set to Live, the live link Driver handheld scanners hosts Delivery photos store, a false positive the rule checks can't see: its sentence says the photos are not kept on the device.
13. Still as `analyst@globex.example`, paste the Acme finding's address from step 3: "Finding not found. This finding doesn't exist or you can't see it." The other org sees none of Acme's findings, documents or decisions.
14. Press Sign out.
